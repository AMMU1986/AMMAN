# Microhardness Distribution — Discussion

The Vickers microhardness traverse across the API X70 SMAW weldment revealed a
markedly heterogeneous distribution governed by the welding thermal cycle
[9, 10]. In the uncharged condition, the heat-affected zone (HAZ) exhibited the
highest hardness (252.6 ± 7.0 HV), followed by the base metal (214.6 ± 5.0 HV),
while the fusion zone recorded the lowest value (196.0 ± 5.7 HV). The elevated
HAZ hardness is attributed to prior-austenite grain coarsening and the formation
of hard bainitic and martensite–austenite (M–A) constituents under the rapid
cooling adjacent to the fusion line [11, 12, 13]. The comparatively soft fusion
zone reflects the predominance of acicular and grain-boundary ferrite promoted
by the basic-coated E9018 deposit, phases intrinsically softer than bainite or
martensite [15, 16]. These gradients agree with the ranges reported for API X70
welds by Hashemi [23], Zhang and Kannan [24], and Kumar and Shahi [25].

Following cathodic hydrogen charging (0.5 M H₂SO₄ + thiourea, 50 mA/cm², 10 h),
every region hardened rather than softened, confirming an electrochemical
hydrogen-induced hardening mechanism in which dissolved hydrogen pins
dislocations and produces solid-solution lattice distortion [32, 33]. The
response was region-dependent: the base metal increased modestly
(≈214.6→220 HV, +2.5 %), the fusion zone moderately (≈196→209 HV, +6.6 %), and
the HAZ most strongly (≈252.6→275 HV, +8.9 %). This HAZ > FZ > BM ranking mirrors
the density of reversible and irreversible hydrogen traps—dislocations,
ferrite-lath interfaces, and M–A/inclusion boundaries—being greatest in the
coarse-grained HAZ [13, 34]. Thiourea further suppressed H₂ recombination,
enhancing atomic-hydrogen uptake and the hardening effect [33]. The non-uniform
surface pitting observed after charging indicates localized hydrogen entry,
producing near-pit hardness perturbations and incipient microcracking [32].

Consequently, the already-hard HAZ not only hardens the most but also becomes the
least tolerant to hydrogen before cracking, identifying it as the critical zone
for hydrogen-assisted failure in API X70 SMAW girth welds [34].
