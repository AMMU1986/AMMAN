### Impact Toughness and Microhardness

The impact-energy and microhardness distributions (Figs. 3 and 4) reveal the strongly heterogeneous and asymmetric character of the dissimilar X70/S355 joint, in which each region responds differently to the single E9018 thermal cycle. The X70 base metal exhibited the highest absorbed energy (≈184 J) together with a moderate hardness of ≈215 HV0.5, consistent with its fine, thermomechanically processed ferrite–acicular-ferrite structure that couples strength with resistance to cleavage [1], [2]. The S355 base metal, a conventional structural grade of higher carbon content, showed the lowest hardness (≈164 HV0.5) yet retained a reasonable toughness (≈127 J), reflecting a softer but ductile ferrite–pearlite matrix.

The weld metal recorded the lowest impact energy of the joint (77.8 ± 5.5 J) despite an intermediate hardness of ≈185–195 HV0.5. This apparent decoupling of hardness and toughness is characteristic of as-deposited multi-pass welds, in which dilution from both dissimilar base metals, elemental partitioning and a coarse columnar solidification structure interrupt the development of a fully fine acicular-ferrite network [5], [7]. Because acicular ferrite nucleates intragranularly on non-metallic inclusions and interlocks to deflect propagating cracks, any dilution-induced shift toward grain-boundary or side-plate ferrite directly depresses weld-metal toughness [11], [12], [13]. The moderate hardness therefore originates from a mixed, partly hardenable microstructure rather than from a toughening one, making the fusion zone the toughness-limiting region of the joint. Notably, the weld-metal energy is only about 42% of that of the X70 base metal and 61% of that of the S355 base metal, quantifying the extent to which the dissimilar dilution penalises weld-metal ductility despite comparable or higher hardness.

The microhardness traverse (Fig. 4) is markedly asymmetric, peaking at ≈248 HV0.5 in the X70-side HAZ immediately adjacent to the fusion line. This peak corresponds to the coarse-grained HAZ (CGHAZ), where the high peak temperature and rapid cooling experienced by the higher carbon-equivalent X70 steel promote bainite and other low-temperature transformation products [3], [6]. The associated X70-side HAZ toughness (≈108 J) is reduced relative to the base metal but remains well above that of the weld metal, indicating that the hard CGHAZ—though embrittled by prior-austenite grain coarsening and probable martensite–austenite (M–A) constituents—is only partially degraded [4]. In contrast, the S355-side HAZ is softer (≈199–202 HV0.5) and exhibits the lowest HAZ toughness (91.8 ± 5.5 J), a consequence of inter-critical and sub-critical softening combined with the inherently leaner hardenability of S355 [6], [10]. This soft band is the classic HAZ-softening zone reported for dissimilar and high-strength steel welds, in which plastic strain preferentially localises [5], [10].

Collectively, the results establish an inverse hardness–toughness relationship within the HAZ but not within the weld metal, underscoring that microstructure—not hardness alone—governs impact resistance in this dissimilar joint. The steep gradient from the ≈248 HV0.5 X70-side CGHAZ to the ≈164 HV0.5 S355 base metal, a fall of roughly 85 HV across the ~12 mm traverse, imposes a pronounced mechanical mismatch that concentrates deformation at the softer S355 interface, mirroring the asymmetric behaviour reported for comparable X70/stainless and X70/SA 516 dissimilar welds [8], [9], [11]. The acicular-ferrite-promoting E9018 consumable moderates, but does not eliminate, this mismatch: the fusion zone remains the weakest link in toughness, so that control of weld-metal dilution and inclusion chemistry is decisive for raising the overall integrity of X70/S355 SMAW joints [12], [13]. These correlations confirm that the combined impact and hardness response, interpreted through the local microstructure, provides a reliable basis for assessing the service suitability of such dissimilar structural–pipeline connections.

### Comparison with previous microstructural studies

The present observations are consistent with, and extend, earlier reports on dissimilar and HSLA weld metallurgy. The acicular-ferrite-bearing fusion zone and the asymmetric HAZ response of the X70/S355 joint closely resemble those documented for dissimilar X70/SA 516 MIG welds, in which the filler chemistry dictated the balance of grain-boundary, polygonal and acicular ferrite and thereby governed the local hardness and toughness [17]. In structural (S355-type) steels, vanadium and related micro-alloying additions have been shown to drive the GMAW HAZ toward ferrite–bainite mixtures containing martensite–austenite (M–A) islands [18], mirroring the hardened X70-side coarse-grained HAZ and the softened S355-side HAZ measured here. Comparative studies of Q355E HSLA welds likewise correlate a higher acicular-ferrite fraction with elevated microhardness and superior impact energy [19], reinforcing the inference that the toughness-limiting weld metal of the present joint could be improved by promoting intragranular acicular-ferrite nucleation. The pronounced interfacial gradients and dilution effects reported for dissimilar X70/super-duplex GTA welds [20], and the local brittle zones that develop in S690QL multi-pass dissimilar weld metal under repeated thermal cycling [21], parallel the heterogeneous fusion-boundary structure of the X70/S355 joint. Finally, investigations of dissimilar stainless–structural steel welds confirm that abrupt hardness steps across the fusion line dominate the mechanical mismatch [22], exactly as reflected in the asymmetric hardness profile of Fig. 4.
