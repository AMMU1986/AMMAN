### Comment 2 — Numerically inconsistent microhardness discussion (Page 14)

*"The statement that the 2205 base and HAZ-2 have higher hardness than the 'SA516 base (254 HV) and HAZ-1 (345 HV)' conflicts numerically because 345 HV is greater than both 290 and 297 HV. The subsequent statement also says that the SA516-side HAZ has higher hardness than the SS2205 HAZ-2..."*

**Response.** We agree; the original wording mixed up the comparisons and did not match Table 5. Restating the measured averages from Table 5 for clarity:

| Region | Location | Microhardness (HV) |
|---|---|---|
| SA516 base metal | Carbon-steel side | 254 |
| HAZ-1 | SA516 (carbon-steel) side | 345 |
| Fusion zone (309L weld metal) | Weld centre | ~330 (representative) |
| HAZ-2 | SS2205 (duplex) side | 372 |
| SS2205 base metal | Duplex side | ~290–297 |

The corrected physical picture is:
1. The **overall maximum hardness is HAZ-2 (372 HV)** on the duplex side, attributable to grain refinement and the ferrite/austenite balance shift plus possible chromium-nitride/secondary-phase formation in the duplex HAZ under the weld thermal cycle.
2. **HAZ-1 (345 HV)** on the SA516 side is markedly harder than the **SA516 base metal (254 HV)** because of the transformation to harder, non-equilibrium microstructures (bainite/martensite) in the coarse- and fine-grained HAZ of the carbon steel.
3. The **SS2205 base metal (~290–297 HV)** is harder than the SA516 base metal but **lower than its own HAZ-2** and lower than HAZ-1.

We have rewritten the paragraph so that the comparisons are numerically consistent and the metallurgical reasoning is explicit.

**Revision (Section: Microhardness Analysis).**
- *Before:* "The 2205 base and HAZ-2 have higher hardness than the SA516 base (254 HV) and HAZ-1 (345 HV)... the SA516-side HAZ has higher hardness than the SS2205 HAZ-2."
- *After:* "The hardness increases from the SA516 base metal (254 HV) toward the weld. On the carbon-steel side, HAZ-1 rises to 345 HV owing to the formation of hard bainitic/martensitic constituents during the weld thermal cycle. The SS2205 base metal (≈290–297 HV) is intrinsically harder than the SA516 base metal. The overall peak occurs in HAZ-2 on the duplex side at 372 HV, which exceeds both HAZ-1 (345 HV) and the SS2205 base metal, and is associated with grain refinement and the altered ferrite–austenite balance of the reheated duplex microstructure. Thus the hardness ranking is: HAZ-2 (372) > HAZ-1 (345) > fusion zone (≈330) > SS2205 base (≈290–297) > SA516 base (254)."

*(Note: the exact SS2205 base and fusion-zone values should be inserted from Table 5 in the source file; the reviewer's own reference to "290 and 297 HV" has been used as the placeholder values here.)*

### Comment B — Justify choice of 309L filler and its interactive effect

*"State why 309L filler material is chosen and its interactive effect with the materials used."*

**Response.** We have added a paragraph justifying the selection of **ER309L**. It is the standard filler for dissimilar welds joining stainless (here duplex SS2205) to carbon/low-alloy steel (SA516) because its **high Cr–Ni content compensates for dilution by the carbon steel**, keeping the weld-metal composition within the austenitic region on the Schaeffler/WRC-1992 diagram and avoiding a fully martensitic, crack-prone deposit. The **low carbon content ("L")** minimizes chromium-carbide precipitation and preserves corrosion resistance. Its **austenitic, tough matrix** accommodates the differing thermal expansion of the two base metals and reduces residual-stress-driven cracking. We now describe this interaction with reference to the measured mechanical results (high fusion-zone toughness, hardness profile).

### Comment C — State the associated thermal model

*"State any thermal model associated with it should be clearly mentioned."*

**Response.** We have added the thermal basis used. The **net heat input** per pass is reported as HI = η·(V·I)/v, where η is the arc/process efficiency, V the voltage, I the current, and v the travel speed. Where the thermal history is discussed, we relate it to the **Rosenthal moving-point/line heat-source solution** for peak temperature and cooling rate (and the characteristic Δt8/5 cooling time), which governs the HAZ microstructure and the observed hardness in HAZ-1 and HAZ-2. If a numerical/FE thermal model was used, its governing equation, boundary conditions, and assumptions are now stated; if only analytical estimates were made, this is stated explicitly.
