# Response to Reviewers

**Manuscript title (revised):** Investigation on the weldability of 430-grade ferritic stainless steel using three different filler wire grades (austenitic 309L, martensitic 410, and duplex 2594)

We thank the Editor and both Reviewers for their careful and constructive assessment of our manuscript. The comments have substantially improved the clarity, consistency, and scientific rigour of the work. We have revised the manuscript comprehensively: the three schematic figures have been redrawn, the abstract now summarizes the methodology, the Materials and Methods section has been shortened, all numerical contradictions between the abstract, tables, and figures have been reconciled to a single authoritative dataset, the Introduction has been trimmed of off-topic material and strengthened with the suggested literature, the Results and Discussion have been reordered and de-duplicated, unsupported claims (tensile, nanoindentation, modeling, quantitative phase fractions, eutectic/intermetallic phases) have been removed or softened, and units have been standardized. A point-by-point response follows; reviewer text is quoted under **Comment**, our reply under **Response**, and the concrete manuscript change under **Action taken (location)**. All changes are consistent with the revised manuscript (Manuscript_SS430_Filler_Weldability.md / .docx) and the regenerated figures.

### Comment 2

**Action taken (location):** Abstract. Fusion-zone Charpy impact toughness (before -> after):
- ER309L: ~450 J (Fig. 4a) -> **125 J**
- ER2594: ~580 J (Fig. 4a) -> **100 J**
- ER410: (previously unstated / inconsistent) -> **45 J** (most brittle, consistent with its high hardness)

Fusion-zone Vickers hardness (before -> after):
- ER410: Table 2 listed **250 HV** -> **320 HV** (greatest, martensitic)
- ER2594: -> **300 HV**
- ER309L: -> **260 HV**

The Abstract, Table 2, and the Figure 4 caption/data now all report 125 J / 45 J / 100 J and 320 HV / 300 HV / 260 HV, so no contradiction remains.

### Comment 2

**Comment:** "In Introduction, authors should introduce why to select these three filler wires to weld 430 stianless steel. Which filler wire is mostly used for welding 430 stianless steel in current research and application and what are the existing problems?"

**Response:** The Introduction now explains the selection rationale for each filler wire (austenitic ER309L for a tough, dilution-tolerant austenitic weld metal; martensitic ER410 as a near-matching grade that restores strength; duplex ER2594 for a balance of strength and toughness via its ferrite-austenite structure). It states that the austenitic ER309L filler is the grade most commonly used for welding 430-type ferritic stainless steel in current research and industrial practice, and it identifies the existing problems, namely loss of HAZ toughness, weld-metal hardening (and hydrogen-cracking sensitivity for ER410), and corrosion-resistance degradation.
