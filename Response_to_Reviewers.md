## Comment B

> *State why 309L filler material is chosen and its interactive effect with the materials used.*

**Response:** We agree that the rationale for selecting 309L required a stronger metallurgical justification. We have expanded the relevant paragraph in the Introduction to explain both the selection criteria and the interactive (dilution) effects with the two base metals.

**Text added (Introduction, 309L filler paragraph):**

> "The 309L filler was selected because its high chromium and nickel equivalents place the diluted weld deposit within the two-phase austenite + ferrite region of the Schaeffler/WRC-1992 constitution diagrams, thereby avoiding a fully martensitic or fully ferritic solidification when it is diluted by the two dissimilar base metals. During welding, the filler interacts with the carbon-rich SA516 on one side and the Cr–Mo–N-rich SS2205 on the other. The elevated Cr and Ni content of 309L buffers the carbon picked up from the SA516 dilution, suppressing the formation of hard, brittle martensite in the fusion zone, while its controlled ferrite content (a small fraction of delta ferrite) mitigates solidification (hot) cracking without promoting excessive sigma-phase formation. The low-carbon 'L' grade further reduces the risk of chromium-carbide precipitation and sensitisation in the weld metal. This combination is consistent with the observed fusion-zone microstructure of skeletal delta ferrite with austenite in the interdendritic regions (Section 3.1)."

*Location of change:* Introduction — paragraph beginning "In current research work, 309L is used as filler wire..."; the interactive effect is also cross-referenced in Section 3.1 (Microstructural Analysis).

## Comment C

> *State any thermal model associated with it should be clearly mentioned.*

**Response:** We agree. The original manuscript discussed cooling rate and thermal cycling only qualitatively. We have added an explicit thermal analysis based on the measured welding parameters.

**Text/analysis added (Experimental Procedure / new subsection "Thermal analysis"):**

- **Net heat input** was calculated from the welding parameters (I = 180 A, V = 18 V, travel speed S = 80 mm/min = 1.333 mm/s) using an arc-transfer efficiency of η ≈ 0.8 for GMAW:

  Q = η × (V × I) / S = 0.8 × (18 × 180) / 1.333 ≈ **1.94 kJ/mm**.

- **Cooling behaviour** has been described using the Rosenthal heat-conduction framework and the Δt8/5 (800 → 500 °C) cooling-time concept, which governs the phase transformations in the two heat-affected zones. The relatively high cooling rate on the SA516 side is consistent with the martensitic tendency and higher hardness observed in HAZ-1, whereas the ferrite/austenite balance retained on the SS2205 side (HAZ-2) is consistent with its more ductile behaviour.

- The calculated heat input and cooling-rate trend are now explicitly linked to the measured microhardness distribution (Table 5, Figure 5) and to the fractography results (Figure 4).

*Location of change:* New short subsection added at the end of the Experimental Procedure (with governing equations and assumed efficiency), cross-referenced in Sections 3.2.2 and the fractography discussion.

## Additional corrections made by the authors (self-identified during revision)

While addressing the above comments, we also corrected the following internal inconsistencies to improve the overall quality of the manuscript:

1. **Conclusion vs. Table 5:** The Conclusion stated that the highest microhardness (372 HV) occurred at "HAZ-1," whereas the Abstract and Table 5 correctly attribute 372 HV to **HAZ-2 (SS2205 side)**. The Conclusion has been corrected for consistency.
2. **"HAZ along X70 side":** The Experimental Procedure erroneously referred to the "X70 side." This has been corrected to the **SA516 side**, as X70 was not used in this study.
3. **Fractography labelling (Figure 4c):** The text stated "Since this region is from the HAZ-1" while describing HAZ-2; this has been corrected.
4. **Filler wire diameter:** The stated "08 mm diameter" 309L filler wire has been corrected to the intended **0.8 mm** diameter, consistent with GMAW practice.
