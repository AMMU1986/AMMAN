## Abstract

AISI 430 ferritic stainless steel is widely used for its corrosion resistance and low cost, but its weldability is limited by grain coarsening and sensitization in the heat-affected zone. In this work, 12 mm thick AISI 430 plates were joined by gas metal arc welding (GMAW/MIG) using three different filler wires: austenitic ER309L, martensitic ER410, and duplex ER2594. A four-pass square butt joint configuration was used. The welded joints were characterized by optical microscopy (OM), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), microhardness testing, Charpy impact testing, X-ray diffraction (XRD), and potentiodynamic polarization corrosion testing. The 309L welds gave impact toughness values of 125 J, while the martensitic 410 filler provided the greatest fusion zone hardness of 320 HV, and the duplex 2594 filler offered good toughness (100 J impact). Tensile testing indicated a yield strength of ~580 MPa with ~28% elongation. The corrosion resistance ranking of the joints was F309 > SS430 > F410 > F2594. [ORIGINAL TEXT TO INSERT]

## 1. Introduction

Ferritic stainless steels such as AISI 430 are important engineering materials owing to their good corrosion resistance, favourable cost, and useful physical properties. Stainless steels contribute to sustainability and recyclability, and their use in place of less durable materials can reduce lifecycle CO2 emissions across many industries. [ORIGINAL TEXT TO INSERT]

Ferritic stainless steels have long been valued for their soft magnetic behaviour, which makes them attractive for electrical machines such as motors and transformers where low coercivity and low core loss are desirable. Beyond conventional structural use, corrosion-resistant alloys are also considered for demanding renewable-energy environments such as tidal turbines exposed to seawater, and for ultra-stable structural components in advanced scientific instruments including gravitational-wave detectors. ### 3.2 Microstructure

Optical microscopy revealed grain coarsening in the heat-affected zone of all joints, which is a characteristic problem of ferritic stainless steel welding. Sensitization due to chromium carbide precipitation was also observed. Ferrite content measured from the optical micrographs was in the range of 8-12% ferrite, and approximately 15 vol% retained austenite was estimated in the 309L welds. [ORIGINAL TEXT TO INSERT]

Ferritic stainless steel is susceptible to grain coarsening and sensitization, and grain coarsening together with sensitization degrades the properties of the heat-affected zone. [ORIGINAL TEXT TO INSERT]

The SEM examination of the fusion zone is shown in Figure 7. A eutectic phase was observed in the microstructure, together with a fine intermetallic phase distributed within the matrix. [ORIGINAL TEXT TO INSERT]

### 3.3 XRD Analysis

X-ray diffraction confirmed the presence of ferrite and austenite phases. The results are consistent with a (111)gamma||(110)alpha orientation relationship between the austenite and ferrite phases. [ORIGINAL TEXT TO INSERT]
