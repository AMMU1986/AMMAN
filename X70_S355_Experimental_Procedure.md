## Experimental Procedure

### Materials and specimen preparation

The materials used in the present study were API 5L X70 micro-alloyed pipeline steel and S355 structural steel, representing a dissimilar pipeline-to-structural combination of practical interest for hybrid steel assemblies. The two steels were received as hot-rolled plates and sectioned into rectangular coupons of 80 mm × 80 mm with a thickness of 12 mm, as shown schematically in Fig. 1. The faying edges of each coupon were machined to a single-V groove with an included angle of 60° ± 5°, a root gap of about 2 mm and a root face of approximately 1.5 mm, in accordance with common practice for 12 mm plate. Prior to welding, the groove faces and the adjacent 25 mm of each plate surface were cleaned with acetone to remove oil, scale and moisture. The chemical compositions of both base metals and of the E9018 filler deposit were determined by atomic absorption spectroscopy (AAS) and are presented in Table 1.

### Welding procedure

The dissimilar X70/S355 joints were produced by shielded metal arc welding (SMAW) in the flat position using a direct-current electrode-positive (DCEP) configuration. A low-alloy E9018 electrode of 2.5 mm diameter was used, the consumable being selected for its low-hydrogen basic coating and its tendency to deposit an acicular-ferrite-rich weld metal. The welding current was maintained at 185 A and the arc voltage at 23 V. The joint was completed in a multi-pass sequence (root, fill and cap passes), with the slag fully removed by chipping and wire brushing between passes to avoid slag entrapment and lack of fusion. For an average travel speed of about 2.5 mm/s, the nominal heat input, H = (60 × V × I)/(1000 × S), was approximately 1.7 kJ/mm per pass; the effective heat input is lower when the arc-transfer efficiency of SMAW (≈0.8) is considered. Identical parameters were applied to every joint so that the dissimilar metallurgical response, rather than the welding condition, governed the measured properties.

### Metallographic and microstructural analysis

Transverse sections spanning the X70 base metal, the fusion zone (FZ) and the S355 base metal were extracted from the welded joints by abrasive cutting under copious coolant to preserve the as-welded microstructure. The sections were hot-mounted, ground on successive SiC papers from 220 to 2000 grit and polished with 3 µm and 1 µm diamond suspensions to a mirror finish. The polished specimens were etched in 2% Nital for 10–15 s to reveal the macro- and microstructure of the base metals, the FZ and the heat-affected zones (HAZ) on both the X70 and S355 sides. Microstructures were examined by optical microscopy, and the constituent phases in each region were identified and correlated with the local mechanical and wear response.

### Impact toughness testing

Charpy V-notch impact tests were carried out at room temperature in accordance with ASTM E23 on standard 10 mm × 10 mm × 55 mm specimens machined transverse to the weld. To resolve the toughness of the heterogeneous joint, the 2 mm V-notch was located separately in the weld metal (WM), in the HAZ on the X70 side and in the HAZ on the S355 side. Three specimens were tested for each notch location, and the absorbed energy is reported as the mean ± standard deviation. The broken halves were retained for fractographic examination.

### Microhardness measurement

The microhardness distribution was measured using the Vickers method in accordance with ASTM E384. A test load of 500 gf (HV0.5) was applied with a dwell time of 10–15 s. Indentations were made along a horizontal traverse at the mid-thickness of the joint, spanning −8 mm to +8 mm about the weld centreline at a regular interval of 0.5 mm, so that the X70 base metal, the X70-side HAZ, the fusion zone, the S355-side HAZ and the S355 base metal were all sampled. The hardness profile was then correlated with the microstructural gradients and the impact and wear behaviour.

### Dry-sliding wear testing

The wear behaviour was evaluated under dry-sliding conditions using a pin-on-disc tribometer following ASTM G99. Cylindrical pins were extracted separately from the X70 base metal, the S355 base metal, the fusion zone and the HAZ regions, and were slid against a hardened steel counterface disc under a constant normal load, sliding speed and sliding distance at ambient temperature. The mass loss of each pin was measured on a precision balance to quantify the wear rate, and the frictional response was recorded throughout each test. The ranking of wear resistance among the weld regions was then interpreted in terms of the local microhardness and microstructure.

### Fractography and worn-surface characterisation

The fracture surfaces of the Charpy specimens and the worn surfaces of the pin-on-disc specimens (X70 base metal, S355 base metal, FZ and both HAZ regions) were examined by scanning electron microscopy on a JEOL IT 500 instrument available at the University Centre for Research and Development (UCRD), Chandigarh University, Mohali, Punjab. The fractographs were used to classify the fracture mode (ductile, brittle or mixed) in each zone, while the worn-surface micrographs identified the operative wear mechanisms—abrasive ploughing, adhesive delamination and oxidative wear—and linked them to the mechanical and microstructural characteristics of each region of the dissimilar joint.
