### Microhardness Profile Analysis

The Vickers microhardness distribution measured along the mid-thickness (1/2T) traverse provides a sensitive indicator of the local microstructural evolution produced by the non-equilibrium thermal cycle of welding, and it varied systematically with the electrode classification. The API X70 base metal exhibited a uniform hardness of 245 HV, consistent with its homogeneous ferrite–pearlite structure, and served as the reference against which the weld regions were assessed. Across all three joints the fusion zone (FZ) recorded the highest hardness, confirming that the as-deposited weld metal over-matched the surrounding regions, a trend that mirrors the microstructure–property relationships reported for multi-pass X70 weldments [21].

The FZ hardness increased progressively with the electrode strength grade, rising from 238 HV for E6018 to 252 HV for E7018 and reaching a maximum of 268 HV for E9018. This escalation is attributed to the increasing proportion of fine, interlocking acicular ferrite together with the higher alloying content of the stronger consumables. The predominantly coarse grain-boundary and polygonal ferrite of the E6018 weld offered the least resistance to indentation, whereas the refined acicular ferrite of the E9018 weld, whose interlocking laths impede dislocation motion and arrest crack propagation, accounts for its superior hardness and toughness [22].

A comparable ranking was observed in the heat-affected zone (HAZ), where hardness values of 225, 232 and 241 HV were recorded for the E6018, E7018 and E9018 joints, respectively. In every case the HAZ was marginally softer than the adjacent FZ, reflecting partial grain coarsening and the formation of lower-hardness transformation products near the fusion line under the applied heat input of 1.44 kJ/mm [23]. The relatively higher HAZ hardness of the E9018 joint indicates that its balanced chemistry and finer transformation products restrained thermal softening even within this metallurgically degraded region, which is otherwise the most vulnerable part of the joint.

The hardness results correlate closely with the corrosion and bend-test findings. The hardest E9018 weld, dominated by acicular ferrite, coincided with the lowest mass loss and the complete absence of intergranular cracking, whereas the softer E6018 weld, associated with coarse grain-boundary structures, proved the most susceptible to attack. This consistent correspondence between elevated microhardness, microstructural refinement and enhanced intergranular corrosion resistance confirms that electrode selection governs both the mechanical and the electrochemical integrity of API X70 SMAW weld joints. Accordingly, the smooth and gradual hardness transition from the base metal through the HAZ to the fusion zone obtained with the E9018 consumable minimises the local property mismatch across the joint, thereby reducing the likelihood of strain localisation and preferential anodic dissolution in service [24].
